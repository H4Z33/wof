## 1. Who owns the RF Online IP, what official service remains, and has anyone enforced against private servers?

### Takeaway
Netmarble has owned the original RF Online IP since buying it from developer CCR in 2020. It shut down the legacy PC game worldwide between 2023 and 2025, and the Taiwan server, closing Dec 31, 2025, was reported as the last one. Netmarble now runs the successor, RF Online Next: Korea launch March 20, 2025, worldwide launch June 16, 2026. I found no public lawsuit, DMCA notice or police action aimed at an RF Online private server or GitHub repo. Korean law does criminalize running private servers commercially, and other publishers (Blizzard, NCSoft) have enforced hard in comparable cases.

## 3. What malware and safety risks come with downloading circulating packages, and what practices reduce them?

### Takeaway
I found no documented case of an RF Online package specifically being backdoored. The wider private-server scene, especially on elitepvpers for Silkroad and Dekaron, has many reports of infected clients (Ramnit, Jeefo, Neshta) and trojaned installers. Packer and anti-cheat components also trigger many false positives. In 2025, large campaigns pushed infostealers through pirated or modded game downloads hosted on MediaFire, which is the same channel many RF releases use. Password-protected archives on external file hosts fit the usual pattern for evading antivirus.

### Cited Findings
- Silkroad threads on elitepvpers say most antivirus flags on private-server files are heuristic, because many servers compress or encrypt files with packers. Detections of Jeefo, Ramnit or Neshta, however, are "NOT normal and WILL cause harm." — [elitepvpers SRO 2024](https://www.elitepvpers.com/forum/sro-private-server/5244747-every-psro-server-contains-viruses-me.html)
- A 2013 elitepvpers report says almost all new Silkroad private-server clients were infected with the Ramnit trojan. — [elitepvpers](https://www.elitepvpers.com/forum/sro-pserver-discussions-questions/2344364-almost-all-new-sro-servers-client-infected-ramnit-trojan.html)
- Threads requesting "clean" server files without trojans show that infected server files are a recurring problem. — [elitepvpers](https://www.elitepvpers.com/forum/sro-private-server/4713388-there-any-server-clean-files-without-any-trojans.html); [elitepvpers](https://www.elitepvpers.com/forum/sro-private-server/3761891-there-viruses-some-server-files.html)
- A Dekaron private server installer was alleged to leave a persistent trojan. — [elitepvpers](https://www.elitepvpers.com/forum/dekaron-private-server/193666-dekaron-extreme-trojan-i-have-proof.html)
- A Metin2 warning says private server operators were stealing users' .cpp files. — [elitepvpers](https://www.elitepvpers.com/forum/metin2-private-server/4970453-warning-how-private-server-aeldra-stealing-your-cpp-files.html)
- Cyderes Howler Cell found "RenEngine" loader malware hidden in modified game launchers spread through piracy and modding sites that redirect to MediaFire ZIPs. It reports over 400,000 infections and more than 5,000 new ones a day, active since at least April 2025. — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/cyderes-howler-cell-threat-research-uncovers-stealer-campaign-targeting-gamers/)
- CyberArk found a StealC and cryptojacking campaign aimed at gamers who cheat, with more than 250 samples and wallets holding over $135K. — [CyberArk](https://www.cyberark.com/resources/threat-research-blog/cheaters-never-win-large-scale-campaign-targets-gamers-who-cheat-with-stealc-and-cryptojacking)
- AhnLab ASEC reported infostealers (Rhadamanthys, LummaC2) disguised as games in 2025. — [AhnLab ASEC](https://asec.ahnlab.com/en/tag/lummac2-en)
- The Chemia early-access game on Steam was compromised to deliver the Vidar infostealer. — [Hackmag](https://hackmag.com/news/chemia-encrypthub)
- Anti-cheat or protection components inside game installers can cause antivirus false positives. A false positive is a clean file flagged because it resembles known malware. — [Wilders Security](https://www.wilderssecurity.com/posts/359505); [F-Secure](https://www.f-secure.com/v-descs/rizo.shtml)
- Infected files have turned up even on legitimate public download servers, for example Mozilla's Korean builds carrying a backdoor-capable virus. — [Securelist](https://securelist.com/infected-files-found-on-mozilla-site/30064)

### Inferences (recommended practices; my synthesis, not taken from a single source)
- **Isolate everything.** Run any downloaded server or client package only inside a disposable VM (Hyper-V, VirtualBox or VMware) with a snapshot taken first. Use host-only networking and no shared folders or clipboard, and never on a machine holding browser sessions, crypto wallets or password stores. Infostealers target exactly those.
- **Test offline first.** Run the RF login, account, zone and DB servers bound to 127.0.0.1 or a host-only network, with outbound internet blocked by firewall rules. Watch for unexpected outbound connections, since a backdoor usually needs to phone home.
- **Hash and scan.** Record SHA-256 hashes of archives and of every executable and DLL. Look them up on VirusTotal by hash rather than uploading, which keeps private files private. Compare hashes across mirrors and check whether an older copy is identical.
- **Read antivirus results carefully.** Generic or heuristic packer detections (Themida, VMProtect, UPX) are common false positives. Named file-infector families (Ramnit, Neshta, Jeefo, Virut) or stealer families (Lumma, StealC, Vidar, Rhadamanthys, RedLine) are real infections and mean the package should be discarded.
- **Treat password-protected archives as a warning sign.** Encryption stops scanners from inspecting the contents, and attackers use this. Scan again after extracting, inside the VM.
- **Watch the database layer.** Many packs ship an old MS SQL Server with default or "sa" credentials and web panels (AppServ, PHP). Never expose these to the internet.
- **Prefer source over binaries.** Source you can read and build yourself is easier to audit than a precompiled launcher or a "defense DLL".
- **Use a sandbox for behaviour.** Tools such as Sysinternals Process Monitor, TCPView and Autoruns, or a sandbox like ANY.RUN or a local CAPE instance, can reveal persistence (scheduled tasks, Run keys, services) after a test run.

## 4. Legal considerations: local personal or educational use versus public hosting (general summary, not legal advice)

### Takeaway
Running leaked server binaries or source copies the publisher's copyrighted code no matter where it runs. Hosting publicly adds distribution, public performance, trademark issues, and in monetized cases large damages or criminal exposure, as in Korea. Purely local, non-commercial use is far less likely to attract enforcement, but that does not make it lawful. The US DMCA §1201 exemption for games whose servers were shut down covers individual play and preservation of lawfully acquired games, but it explicitly excludes persistent-world MMOs and does not authorize using leaked server code. Clean-room reimplementations sit on firmer ground but still face EULA and anti-circumvention risk (Blizzard v. bnetd).

### Cited Findings
- The US Copyright Office's §1201 exemption covers video games "for which outside server support has been discontinued," for individual play by gamers and preservation by libraries, archives and museums. It does not cover MMOs with persistent worlds. Online games were added in the 2018 rulemaking, and renewal petitions exist for the 2027 cycle. — [EFF](https://www.eff.org/let-game-fans-keep-abandoned-games-running); [Copyright Office 2027 renewal petition](https://www.copyright.gov/1201/2027/petitions/renewal/Renewal-Pet-Abandoned-Video-Games-Ken-Austin.pdf)
- The ESA opposed letting games be modified to connect to new servers after publisher support ends. — [EFF](https://www.eff.org/node/99817)
- In Blizzard v. bnetd (8th Cir. 2005), the court held that the defendants were bound by EULA and click-wrap terms that banned reverse engineering. It found that their Battle.net emulator violated those terms and the DMCA's anti-circumvention provisions, and rejected the interoperability defense. — [EFF case page](https://www.eff.org/cases/blizzard-v-bnetd); [EFF Deeplinks](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2005/09/courts-dmca-you-can-repair-products-you-cant-improve-them)
- Blizzard v. Reeves (Scapegaming, 2010) produced an $88M default judgment for a monetized WoW private server, on copyright, unfair competition and circumvention claims. — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2010-08-14-private-server-company-forced-to-pay-blizzard-88-million.html)
- Korea defines an illegal private server as gaining benefit by running similar or identical games without the copyright holder's permission. The penalty is up to 5 years or 50M KRW plus confiscation. — [Daeryun Law](https://www.daeryunlaw.com/ch/faq/detail/939)
- Korean enforcement in practice: 61 people punished and 5 jailed out of about 177K servers detected in five years. — [fnnews](https://en.fnnews.com/news/202510090023300820)

### Inferences
- **Risk gradient,** from lowest to highest:
  1. Analyzing or studying the protocol privately without running leaked code.
  2. Running a personal offline instance, just you on localhost.
  3. A private LAN or friends server.
  4. A public free server.
  5. A public monetized server (donations, cash shop).
  
  Enforcement history (Scapegaming, NCSoft/Lineage, Korean prosecutions) concentrates almost entirely on the monetized end.
- The legacy RF client also needs a lawfully obtained copy. The official downloads are gone, so most clients circulating now are redistributions, which is copyright infringement on the distributor's side.
- Even after official service ended, Netmarble keeps copyright and trademark rights. Running RF Online Next commercially makes "abandonware" arguments weak for the brand.
- The rules differ by country (EU Software Directive art. 6 interoperability decompilation, Korean GIPA, Brazilian and Indonesian copyright law). Anyone with real exposure should get local legal advice.

- I found no RF Online EULA text, past or present, to quote its reverse-engineering or private-server clauses.

## 5. Legitimate or legal alternatives

### Takeaway
There is no official offline mode, and no official legacy RF Online service exists any more. The only official way to play RF in 2026 is RF Online Next. Open-source clean-room RF emulators exist only as immature projects (for example vasyater/dooa). Most "emulators" and file packs on RaGEZone come from leaked official binaries or source.

### Cited Findings
- All current legacy RF Online services are private servers, and no official legacy service remains. — [GameIndustry.ph](https://www.gameindustry.ph/article/rf-online)
- RF Online Next is the official successor: Korea in 2025, Japan announced for 2025, global on PC and mobile on June 16, 2026, with a "Nova" super-boost server pre-registration in Korea. — [Gamespress](https://www.gamespress.com/RF-ONLINE-NEXT-TO-LAUNCH-GLOBALLY-ON-June-16); [GameMeca](https://www.gamemeca.com/view.php?gid=1781247)
- vasyater/dooa is an MIT-licensed RF Online emulator that is still in development ("coming soon"), and is the only clean-room-style candidate found. — [GitHub](https://github.com/vasyater/dooa)
- Other GitHub RF projects are mods, data tables or defense DLLs built on official server binaries, not independent servers. — [Kohaku-Dev](https://github.com/Nur1Labs/Kohaku-Dev-RF-Online-); [Yorozuya](https://github.com/goodwinxp/Yorozuya)
- RaGEZone RF "source code" threads are leaked official code, including client, server and R3 engine, or rebuilt from it. — [RaGEZone reupload](https://forum.ragezone.com/threads/release-reupload-rf-online-sourcecode-client-server-engine.1056553/); [RaGEZone 2025](https://forum.ragezone.com/threads/rf-online-source-code.1253727/)
- For comparison, mature open-source emulators for other MMOs exist, such as ModernUO for Ultima Online and Erupe for Monster Hunter Frontier. — [ModernUO](https://github.com/modernuo/modernuo); [Erupe](https://github.com/Mezeporta/Erupe)

### Inferences
- The "GoMMO Emulator by OSO" (4.15, 4.55, 2.2.3.2) may be a reimplementation, but its provenance and licence are unverified. It should not be assumed clean-room or safe.
- The cleanest legal path for learning would be to write your own server from protocol observation, use only your own code and assets, and not distribute Netmarble's client or data. Even this carries EULA and §1201 risk under bnetd-style reasoning in the US.
